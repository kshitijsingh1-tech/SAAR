# Agricultural Pathology & Soil Water Management Field Guide (FAO Standard)

## Section 1: Soil Moisture & Plant Transpiration Stress
Soil moisture depletion below 30% available water capacity initiates stomatal closure in most crop varieties.
Prolonged soil moisture deficit (< 25%) leads to reduced nutrient uptake (specifically Nitrogen and Magnesium), resulting in leaf chlorosis (yellowing) starting from lower leaves.
Typical lag time between soil moisture drop below 25% and visible leaf yellowing is 3 to 7 days.

## Section 2: Nitrogen & Fertilizer Dynamics
Nitrogen application (NPK fertilizer) increases chlorophyll synthesis within 48 to 72 hours under adequate soil moisture conditions (> 35%).
If fertilizer is applied during severe water deficit (< 20% soil moisture), root burn can occur, accelerating leaf tip necrosis and stress scores.

## Section 3: Temperature & Heat Deficit
Ambient temperatures exceeding 32°C combined with relative humidity below 50% increase vapor pressure deficit (VPD).
High VPD accelerates evapotranspiration, causing temporary wilting even when soil moisture appears marginally adequate.

## Section 4: Pest & Disease Interactions
High leaf yellowing paired with localized lesions often indicates fungal pathogens (e.g., leaf spot or rust) rather than pure water stress.
Fungal spore germination is favored by high relative humidity (> 80%) and leaf surface moisture duration > 6 hours.
