# Badminton Biomechanics & Stroke Kinematics Knowledge Base

## 1. Contact Height & Downward Smash Angle
In competitive badminton overhead strokes, contact point elevation is the single most critical geometric determinant of downward attacking steepness and opponent defensive difficulty:
- **Apex Contact Height**: Maximizing vertical impact height allows an attacking smash angle of downward descent between $-15^\circ$ and $-25^\circ$, forcing opponent lifts rather than counter-drives.
- **Elbow Extension Angle at Contact**:
  - *Sub-optimal ($< 125^\circ$)*: Premature deceleration, compressed lever arm, and flatter trajectory prone to mid-court interception.
  - *Competent ($125^\circ - 145^\circ$)*: Functional contact height with adequate compliance for deception.
  - *Optimal ($145^\circ - 165^\circ$)*: Near-complete arm extension at highest attainable apex point without joint hyperextension.

## 2. Forearm Pronation vs. Premature Wrist Flexion
High-velocity overhead strokes (smash, clear, attacking drive) derive primary terminal racket speed from rapid internal shoulder rotation and forearm pronation rather than isolated wrist flexion:
- **Forearm Pronation Contribution**: Accounts for up to 40–50% of resultant racket-head velocity immediately prior to shuttle contact.
- **Risk of Isolated Wrist Snapping**: Relying exclusively on wrist palmar flexion ("wrist snap") produces premature deceleration, reduced shot power, and chronic micro-trauma to the dorsal wrist retinaculum and flexor carpi ulnaris tendon.

## 3. Scissor Kick & Lower-Body Force Attenuation
The rear-court scissor kick (hitch kick) enables mid-air pelvic rotation and rapid post-stroke recovery toward the central base:
- **Pelvic Rotation**: Initiates proximal acceleration, rotating pelvis through $60^\circ - 90^\circ$ relative to the baseline.
- **Landing Force Dissipation**: Racket-side leg lands behind the non-racket leg to brake angular momentum. Knee flexion upon landing should maintain $130^\circ - 150^\circ$ to attenuate ground reaction forces (often $2.5 - 3.5\times$ body weight) and protect the patellar tendon.

## 4. Overhead Clear vs. Drop Shot Biomechanical Consistency
Tactical deception relies on matching preparation kinematics between attacking smashes, fast clears, and slice drops:
- **Preparation Invariance**: Trunk rotation, racket cocking, and elbow elevation should remain indistinguishable up to 50ms prior to impact.
- **Deceleration Timing in Drop Shots**: Racket head deceleration in slice/reverse-slice drop shots should occur within the final 20–30ms before contact, utilizing controlled forearm supination/pronation rather than visible body relaxation.
