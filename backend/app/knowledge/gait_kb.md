# Pediatric Gait & Toddler Movement Analysis

## Developmental Milestones in Toddler Gait
Most children begin walking independently between 9 and 18 months according to World Health Organization (WHO) motor development benchmarks. During the first few years of independent walking, pediatric gait characteristics undergo rapid maturation. Sutherland's developmental gait studies establish that mature adult-like gait patterns typically emerge around 3 to 4 years of age. Early toddler gait is characterized by a wide base of support, short and rapid steps, flat-foot or toe-initial contact rather than mature heel-to-toe roll, and higher step-to-step variability.

## Cadence and Step-Frequency Reference Bands
Cadence represents the number of steps taken per minute. In developing toddlers, cadence decreases progressively with neurological maturation and limb elongation:
- **12 to 18 Months**: Typical reference cadence is 150 to 190 steps/minute.
- **18 to 24 Months**: Typical reference cadence is 135 to 175 steps/minute.
- **24 to 36 Months**: Typical reference cadence is 120 to 160 steps/minute.
- **36 to 48 Months**: Typical reference cadence is 115 to 145 steps/minute.
- **48 Months and Older**: Typical reference cadence stabilizes around 110 to 135 steps/minute.
These ranges are synthesized from Rygelova et al. (PLOS ONE 2023), Sutherland, and Dusing & Thorpe GAITRite pediatric norms for contextual reference and parent education, not diagnostic labeling.

## Temporal Step Symmetry
Left-to-right step-time symmetry measures the balance between left and right limb support and swing durations. Symmetry percentage difference is defined as:
$$\text{Asymmetry}\% = \frac{100 \cdot |\text{StepTime}_{\text{Left}} - \text{StepTime}_{\text{Right}}|}{0.5 \cdot (\text{StepTime}_{\text{Left}} + \text{StepTime}_{\text{Right}}) + \varepsilon}$$
In typical pediatric movement, left and right step times are balanced within a 10% difference threshold. Transient minor asymmetry can occur due to footwear, surface unevenness, or turning. Persistent elevated asymmetry across repeated assessments warrants observation by a pediatric physical therapist.

## Step Rhythm Consistency and Variability
Step-time Coefficient of Variation (CoV %) quantifies the stride-to-stride temporal consistency:
$$\text{CoV}\% = \frac{100 \cdot \text{Standard Deviation}}{\text{Mean Step Time}}$$
In mature walking, step-time CoV is typically below 5–8%. In toddlers (under 3 years), motor coordination is developing, and step-time variation up to 15% is commonly observed as motor patterns stabilize. Higher variability indicates developing motor control and balance strategies.

## Non-Diagnostic Screening & Safety Policy
Video-based pose analysis serves strictly as non-diagnostic movement observation and developmental screening. Measured values represent deterministic temporal observations from video recordings. Clinical diagnoses such as cerebral palsy, developmental delay, or hip dysplasia require comprehensive physical examination, clinical history, and specialized instrumented gait laboratory evaluation by certified pediatric specialists.
