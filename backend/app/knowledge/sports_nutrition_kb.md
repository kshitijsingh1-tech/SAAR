# General Sports Nutrition & Athletic Recovery Knowledge Base

## 1. Load-Tied Fluid & Electrolyte Replenishment
In intermittent court sports such as badminton, sweat rates typically range from $0.8\text{ L/hr}$ to $1.8\text{ L/hr}$ depending on indoor humidity, ambient temperature, and rally intensity:
- **During-Play Hydration Benchmark**: For active training sessions exceeding 30 minutes, an intake of **400–800 mL per hour** of cool water or hypotonic electrolyte solution is recommended to prevent voluntary dehydration ($>2\%$ body mass deficit).
- **Electrolyte Sodium Balance**: In prolonged sessions exceeding 60 minutes with heavy sweat rates, electrolyte solutions containing **300–600 mg/L sodium** maintain plasma volume and reduce exercise-associated muscle cramping risk.

## 2. Carbohydrate Periodization Tied to Session Duration & Intensity
Endogenous muscle and liver glycogen stores are the primary substrate fueling high-intensity repeated lunges, smashes, and explosive accelerations:
- **Short-Duration Sessions ($< 45\text{ minutes}$)**: Rinsing with water or modest carbohydrate intake is sufficient; glycogen depletion is not the primary limiting factor.
- **Moderate to High-Load Sessions ($45 - 90\text{ minutes}$, $\ge 300\text{ kcal}$)**: Consumption of **30–60 grams per hour** of rapidly digestible carbohydrates (e.g., maltodextrin, glucose-electrolyte beverage) maintains blood glucose levels and motor decision-making accuracy late in sets.
- **Post-Exercise Glycogen Resynthesis**: Ingesting **1.0–1.2 g/kg body weight of carbohydrates** within the initial 2 hours post-session accelerates glycogen replenishment following high-volume training.

## 3. Post-Training Protein Window & Musculoskeletal Recovery
- **Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS)**: Consuming **20–30 grams of high-biological-value protein** (containing approximately 2.5–3.0g leucine) within 45–90 minutes following intense sessions stimulates myofibrillar protein remodeling and attenuates exercise-induced muscle soreness (DOMS).
- **Recovery Timing**: Distributing protein evenly across 3–4 hour intervals throughout the day optimizes chronic neuromuscular adaptation.

## 4. Strict Non-Diagnostic Disclosure (Section 23 & 52)
- **General Guidance Only**: Nutritional parameters provided by this system represent general, evidence-grounded sports science guidelines scaled solely by observed session duration and estimated metabolic load (MET $\times$ duration).
- **Scope Limitations**: Video motion analysis provides zero physiological biomarker, blood serum, or tissue metabolite data. Motion analysis algorithms cannot evaluate clinical medical status or individual biochemical markers.
